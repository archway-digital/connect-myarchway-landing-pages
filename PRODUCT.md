## Users

**Primary buyer.** Independent insurance agency owners running 2–50 staff agencies on Applied Epic, AMS360, HawkSoft, EZLynx, Vertafore, Sagitta, or TAM. They are not technologists. They evaluate technology through operational risk, staff time, and carrier requirements — not features or specs.

**Secondary buyer.** Operations managers, office managers, or compliance leads at larger agencies.

**Context.** Inbound from email, SEO, or BDR outreach. Skeptical of vendor follow-up because they have been burned before. They want a real, useful asset (guide, checklist, assessment) before any sales conversation. They respond to operational pain stated in agency terms, platform-specific credibility, and peer proof.

## Product Purpose

Landing pages are **revenue infrastructure**, not awareness assets. Each page exists to move anonymous traffic into named, qualified pipeline via HubSpot. Form fills determine MQL volume. MQL volume determines BDR workload. The conversion mechanism — not the brand impression — is what success is measured by.

Three verticals share the same landing-page system: **IT & Cybersecurity**, **AI & Automation**, **Digital Marketing**. See the "What We Sell" section below for vertical-specific posture.

## Brand Personality

**Three words: knowing, plainspoken, useful.**

Voice signals from existing email copy (Brad Ruben, SueAnn Priem, Hannah, Sam):

- First-person and personal. Real people are named. Customer-facing copy reads like a note from a colleague who knows the business.
- Conversational and self-aware. Self-deprecating humor is welcome ("losing to someone pushing sixty is tough on the ego"). Never stiff, never corporate.
- Insurance-specific credibility worn lightly. AMS platforms are named by name. Carrier audits, NYDFS, MFA mandates are referenced without explaining what they are.
- Practical first, sales last. "No fluff. Just the basics" and "No spam. No sales call unless you want one. Just the guide." are house lines.
- Calm authority on scary topics (cyber, compliance). Fear is never the lead. The agency is treated as competent; we are the partner that has seen this 500 times.

Tone calibrators across verticals:

- **IT & Cybersecurity:** calm authority. Proactive, not reactive. Frame problems in agency operational terms.
- **AI & Automation:** skepticism validation first. Anchor every claim to a named workflow (renewal drafting, certificate generation, commission reconciliation). Never hype.
- **Digital Marketing:** practical diagnosis. The website was built to exist, not to convert. Diagnostic language, not aspirational.

## Anti-references

Do **not** look or sound like:

- **Generic cybersecurity SaaS.** Dark blue + network nodes + glowing locks. (This is the single biggest trap the current site falls into.)
- **Generalist IT MSP corporate sites.** All-things-to-all-people. Buzzwords, slick gradients, no personality.
- **Insurance trade-association sites.** Stock photos of handshakes, vague "protecting what matters" copy, cluttered nav.
- **Generic agency/marketing sites.** Pastel gradients, oversized hero photos, "we help you grow."

What we are not:

- Not a SaaS marketing page. No feature comparison grids, capability lists, or pricing tables.
- Not generic B2B. "Grow your business," "unlock your potential," "transform your operations" do not exist here.
- Not compliance theater. Fear-based urgency erodes trust with this audience.
- Not AI hype. Every AI page leads with skepticism validation, not enthusiasm.
- Not awareness content. These pages exist to convert.

## Design Principles

1. **Specialist first, every page.** "We work exclusively with independent insurance agencies on IT, cybersecurity, marketing, and AI" is a qualification filter that belongs on every page. Visual choices should reinforce specialist-not-generalist within the first scroll.
2. **Practice what we preach.** We sell websites that convert; our own pages must convert. We sell calm cybersecurity advice; our pages must read calm, not alarmist. The medium is the message.
3. **Conversion mechanics over creative flourish.** Form placement, trust-line copy, post-download motion are first-order concerns. Design decisions that hurt MQL volume are wrong regardless of how they look.
4. **Show the people.** Brad, SueAnn, Hannah, Sam are the brand. Bylined content, real names, and human voice beat anonymous "our team" framing.
5. **Diagnose, then prescribe.** Name the specific operational risk before naming the solution. "Outdated systems. Unvalidated backups. Security settings nobody touched in two years." beats "Modernize your IT."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA minimum** across all pages. AAA where reasonable on legal/footer text.
- **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Many agency owners are 50+; vestibular accommodation matters.
- **No JS-only content.** Pages must remain functional if scripts fail or load late.
- **Touch targets ≥ 44 × 44 px.** Nav, footer, social, CTA buttons.
- **Heading hierarchy.** One H1 per page, no level skips.
- **Color is never the only signal.** Form errors, status, links must also use shape, weight, or text.

## How We Sell

### The Funnel Motion

Every landing page sits at the top of the same revenue motion:

```
Inbound traffic (email, SEO, BDR outreach)
  -> Landing Page
    -> Form fill -> MQL created in HubSpot
      -> Post-download nurture sequence (3 emails)
        -> BDR follow-up on high-intent signals
          -> Discovery or Scoping Call
            -> Deal opened
              -> Closed Won -> Expansion
```

Landing pages are not awareness assets. They are the conversion mechanism that moves anonymous traffic into named, qualified pipeline. Form fills determine MQL volume. MQL volume determines BDR workload. This is not design work — it is revenue infrastructure.

### Page Types and Their Role

**Lead Magnet Pages (gated PDF downloads)**
The primary acquisition tool. A contact provides name, email, and agency name in exchange for a practical guide, checklist, or assessment. The asset must deliver real value — not a thinly veiled product pitch. Trust is built on asset quality. The post-download sequence and BDR follow-up do the conversion work.

**Booking Pages**
Direct path to a meeting. Used when the audience segment already has a defined problem and the friction of a form-then-nurture sequence is unnecessary. Copy focuses on what happens in the call, not on Archway's credentials.

**Tool Pages**
Interactive tools (website grader, AI readiness assessments) that gate results behind a form. Two-step flow: contact info first, tool output second. The tool result IS the value delivery. BDR follow-up triggers on contacts who view results and visit a booking page.

**Re-engagement Pages**
Minimal friction. Used for cold or disengaged segments. Goal is any engagement signal that moves a contact from a lower list tier into active nurture in HubSpot.

### HubSpot Integration (Portal ID: 6869933)

All landing pages must connect to HubSpot. On every form submit:
- Contact created or updated
- Lifecycle stage set to MQL
- Vertical interest property (archway_vertical_interest) updated
- BDR task created if a booking page is visited within 7 days without a meeting scheduled

Form submissions that do not fire HubSpot actions are not functional pages — they are data dead ends.

## What We Sell and How It Maps to Pages

### IT & Cybersecurity

**What we sell:** Security assessments, compliance reviews, penetration testing, Microsoft 365 and cloud migrations, Azure AD deployments, managed IT, endpoint protection, incident response planning.

**Funnel role:** Highest deal volume vertical. Compliance pressure and carrier mandate requirements create natural urgency without requiring fear-based copy. The most reliable and consistent pipeline driver across all contact segments.

**Landing page posture:** Calm authority. Identify the specific operational risk — carrier audit exposure, ransomware gap, dependency on one staff member knowing where everything is. Position Archway as the partner that runs IT the way an insurance agency actually operates.

**Page-to-deal motion:** Lead magnet or booking page -> compliance review call -> security proposal or project scoping -> Closed Won -> managed IT recurring path.

### AI & Automation

**What we sell:** AMS workflow automation, document generation implementations, commission reconciliation automation, SharePoint AI builds, AI voice and chatbot implementations, workflow audits.

**Funnel role:** Growing vertical. Higher education requirement than IT. Longer qualification cycle. Strongest when anchored to a specific workflow problem the agency already knows they have — not a general AI conversation.

**Landing page posture:** Skepticism validation first. Most agency owners have seen AI demos that did not deliver. Acknowledge that. Lead with what is actually working at agencies like theirs, not with what AI can theoretically do. Name the AMS platform whenever possible.

**Page-to-deal motion:** Lead magnet (AI guide, AMS workflow guide) -> nurture sequence -> AI or SharePoint consult booking -> scoping call -> implementation project -> ongoing advisory path.

### Digital Marketing

**What we sell:** Website redesign, local SEO, website maintenance retainers, email marketing setup, review strategy, agency branding.

**Funnel role:** Lower volume than IT but a strong expansion signal for existing IT customers. The website grader tool is the highest-leverage interactive entry point — it creates an immediate, personalized result that names the problem.

**Landing page posture:** Practical diagnosis. The agency's website was built to exist, not to convert. Load speed, local search visibility, trust signals, contact form placement — these are fixable problems most owners do not know they have because no one has looked at their site from the outside.

**Page-to-deal motion:** Website grader or marketing guide -> results-based follow-up -> website redesign or SEO project conversation -> Closed Won -> maintenance retainer or ongoing digital marketing.

## Positioning Rules (All Pages)

### The Core Credibility Line

"We work exclusively with independent insurance agencies on IT, cybersecurity, marketing, and AI."

This belongs on every page. It is not a tagline — it is a qualification filter. When an agency owner reads it, they immediately understand Archway is not a generalist vendor guessing at their world. Use it in the authority section of every lead magnet and booking page.

### Vertical-Specific Positioning

**IT & Cybersecurity**
- Frame problems in agency operational terms, not technical terms
- Reference carrier requirements, NYDFS, MFA mandates where applicable
- "Proactive" beats "reactive" — position Archway as the practice that prevents the crisis, not the one called after it happens

**AI & Automation**
- Validate skepticism before selling anything
- Connect every AI application to a named workflow problem (certificate generation, renewal follow-up, commission reconciliation, AMS data entry)
- The positioning frame is "what is actually working at independent agencies right now" — not "the future of insurance"

**Digital Marketing**
- Lead with what the agency is losing, not what they could gain
- Site performance, local search ranking, and trust signals are diagnosable — use diagnostic language, not aspiration language
- The website grader is a proof-of-competence tool, not just a lead magnet

### AMS Platform Name-Dropping

Naming specific platforms signals that Archway understands the agency's actual environment. Use these on any page that is IT or AI-adjacent:

Applied Epic — AMS360 — HawkSoft — EZLynx — Vertafore — Sagitta — TAM

This is not filler. It is one of the fastest credibility signals available with this audience.

## Post-Download Sequence Logic

Every gated download triggers a 3-email nurture sequence:

- **Email 1 (Immediate):** Deliver the asset. Direct them to the most actionable section. No ask.
- **Email 2 (Day 3):** Education only. Deepen the specific topic. No CTA pressure.
- **Email 3 (Day 7):** Soft booking invitation. "Ready to talk through what makes sense for your agency?"

BDR follow-up activates on contacts who download and visit a booking page within 7 days without scheduling. These are the highest-intent contacts in the funnel and should be worked within 24-48 hours.
